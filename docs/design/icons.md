# Making a Zimi icon

Everything here is reproduced from numbers by `python3 scripts/icons/build.py` (run from the repo root); read this page first, then run it, then change one thing at a time.

## What the script does

`build.py` writes `mark.svg`, `zimigames.svg` and `almanac.svg` into `--out DIR` (default a temp dir), and the output is byte-identical from run to run. `--apply` rewrites `zimi/builtin/games/mark.svg` and the generated `ZMARK` block in `zimi/builtin/games/cards.js`; `--icons` rasterises both ZIM icons to 96x96 RGBA PNGs with transparent corners through headless Chrome and ImageMagick; `--check` fails if the repo drifted from the generator; `--review` writes a board of both icons at 1024, 96, 48 and 32. After `--apply` or `--icons` the own ZIMs need rebuilding (`python3 -m zimi.ownzims`, and `--check` must be clean). The helpers live beside it: `astro.py` (Sun and Moon), `glitter.py` (sea glitter), `splat.py` (the smooth closed curve), `zglyph.py` (the Z from the font) and `findfont.py` (how the font was identified).

## The mark

The mark is our Z on a river pebble, and the Z is rendered from the font, never traced. It is SF Compact at its default instance (wght 1000, Black), glyph "Z", straight from `/System/Library/Fonts/SFCompact.ttf`; it is the same Z as `zimi/assets/icon.png`, and it is not SF Pro Bold, which Eric called the thin one. `python3 scripts/icons/zglyph.py` extracts the outline with fontTools and checks it against `ZG` in `build.py`. The outline is twelve points on a 2048 em with y up: (110,0) (1184,0) (1184,326) (611,326) (611,346) (1161,1103) (1161,1365) (115,1365) (115,1039) (660,1039) (660,1020) (110,262). The coordinates are fixed because they are the font's own and the app icon already ships this glyph; changing one by a unit makes the family drift, so a different look means a different decision, not an edit to `ZG`. The Z is set the way the app icon sets it: font size 0.72 of its box, glyph bounds centred, box `ZB` = 31 units on the 100-unit icon, centred on the pebble.

The pebble is a smooth, convex, slightly worn river stone, centre (19.4, 18.2) on the 100-unit icon, radii 15.0 by 12.6, tilted -8 degrees. It is an ellipse radius scaled by k(t) = 1 + 0.028 cos(2t + 0.9) + 0.014 sin(3t + 0.3) - 0.012 cos(t - 0.6), sampled at 40 points, with the underside flattened by 0.6 times max(0, sin t) cubed, and joined by a closed Catmull-Rom spline (`splat.catmull`). Only low harmonics, so it stays convex and never reads as a blob or a shape. The Z's gradient runs corner to corner of the Z's box through #f59e0b, #f97316 (at half) and #ef4444. The stone's fill runs top to bottom through #1f1a17, #0d0b0a (at 55 percent) and #070605, which is a warm near-black so it reads as a stone and not a sticker. Under it is a soft drop shadow (dx 0, dy 1.6, blur 1.6, black at 0.45), and around it a faint edge, a white stroke 0.6 wide at 0.10 opacity. The mark alone is `mark.svg`, viewBox `2 2 36 33`; it holds at 22 to 24 px with its shadow, so there is no flatter small variant.

## The icon grammar

An icon is a 1024 canvas (drawn in a 100-unit viewBox) clipped to a rounded square with rx 22.4, with a soft vignette (radial, clear to 55 percent, black at 0.42 at the edge) and the mark on top as an island near the top-left, so the art shows on every side of it. The subject is large and centred as a group, not pushed aside to make room for the mark; the pebble overlays the art. There is one light, from the upper left, and every object shades and casts its shadow from it. Colour comes from the product: ZimiGames is a green felt table (#23895a to #0f4a30 to #06261a), the Almanac is dusk over the sea. Keep the style flat-ish vector with gentle gradients and smooth edges, no outlines.

## The suits and the Ace of Spades

The four suit pips in `cards.js` (`SUIT_SHAPES`) are one family at card-maker proportions, each in a 20-unit box: the spade has a crisp cusp, symmetric lobes and a slim flared stem; the club reuses the spade's stem under three lobes round a filled centre; the heart is slimmer than a valentine, with a shallow cleft and a pointed tip; the diamond has gently bowed sides and is a little wider than a rhombus so it carries the weight of the others. Draw them together and check them side by side at corner-index size and at ace size, since one fat or lumpy suit makes the whole deck look wrong. The Ace of Spades carries the maker's mark, as real decks do: the Z alone, in its gradient, 9 units tall in the spade's belly (centre 0.3 units below the middle) with clear margins to the outline, and at tableau size the same Z in the small spade. No pebble on the Ace: we tried a stamp below the spade, the pebble inset in the belly and a subtle pebble behind the Z, and Eric chose the Z alone, as large as the margins allow. The Zimi card back carries the same Z, drawn from the same numbers.

## The physics rules we learned the hard way

ZimiGames is one scene with one camera and one light, and every object follows from them. The camera is a true pinhole 700 mm from the point it looks at, at 58 degrees elevation, looking along +y; each object is projected through the same function, never drawn by eye. The light is 58 degrees up, from the left and a little behind, so shadows fall to the right and slightly toward the viewer, longer for taller things; shadows are projected from the real geometry, sharp at contact and softer with height. The derivation is in the comments at the top of the games block in `build.py`.

A die is right-handed (a Western die: 1, 2 and 3 meet counter-clockwise at a corner), opposite faces sum to 7, and the code asserts both; the three visible faces must be mutually adjacent. Pips are circles on their face planes, projected through the camera, so the side pips foreshorten with the face and are never smeared blobs; the top 5 is a true quincunx on that face's grid. The die is one rounded cube tessellated through its flat faces and rounded rim, each patch shaded from its own normal, not chamfers with gradient strips, which look lumpy. A card lies flat in the table plane. A joystick's shaft axis runs through the centre of the ball, the stick stands up out of a washer and boot, the shaft is one chrome cylinder with one smooth highlight (no hard banding), and the stick casts one shadow, a single chain of rings from foot to ball that softens with height and matches the light. The ball's highlight sits on the half vector between the light and the camera. Do not move the card or the die when fixing the stick: the fit scale is frozen in `build.py` on purpose.

## The Almanac rules

The Almanac icon is a real chart, so it has to be astronomically right. It is the Sun's altitude through one real day in San Francisco (37.77 N, 122.42 W), the solstice arcs, the Moon's path, and one line through the Moon and the Sun at the golden hour (the Sun 6 degrees up). The positions come from a real ephemeris, not eyeballing: `astro.py` is Schlyter's low-precision Sun and Moon (good to about a tenth of a degree, plenty for an icon), with the Moon's topocentric parallax applied. The Moon's lit side points at the Sun, so `bright_limb_angle` builds the Moon-to-Sun direction in a local East, North, Up frame (right-handed), projects it into the Moon's tangent plane and measures it against local up; it is the on-screen direction, not the position angle from north.

The sea carries the Sun's glitter, computed from Cox and Munk (1954): facet slopes are Gaussian with variance 0.003 + 0.00512 W for wind W m/s (5 here), radiance goes as the slope density over 4 cos^4(tilt) cos(view zenith), so it is brightest in a thin band at the horizon, then a column of glints that fades and widens downward. The Sun is drawn as an extended source, averaged over its disc as drawn (`glitter.glints_ext`), not a point; the drawn Sun is the one enlargement, since at true size it would be a quarter of a unit across. The date is locked on 23 March 2026 (`DAY`, override only with `ALM_DAY` to explore): a waxing crescent, 31 percent lit, high in the evening sky, near the equinox, with a Moon that reads at small sizes. We checked birthdays (20 April 2023, a new Moon that sits on the Sun as a dark disc; 18 April 2025, a waning gibbous that is under the sea at sunset and on top of the Sun at sunrise); each is accurate and each reads worse at icon size, so the icon stays on March. Do not change the art or the date.

## The review loop

Nothing goes to Eric before you have looked at it yourself. Render at 1024, then 96, 48 and 32 (`--review` makes the board), crop at 4x on the die, the stick, the Z and every edge, and look at each crop with your own eyes; counting things in the DOM or trusting the maths is not looking. Check it on the real surfaces too: the back button at 24 px, a sheet heading at 22 px and the shelf header at 390 px in WebKit, light and dark. Then get a second pair of eyes (a reviewer agent briefed with the camera and light numbers and asked for every physical inconsistency), fix what is real, and look again. Eric reviews on his phone, where he zooms in, so what survives 4x zoom on a phone screenshot is the bar. Iterate on one flaw at a time, keep the parts he approved exactly where they are, and never use em dashes in anything you write about it.

## A note on the font's licence

SF Compact is Apple's font, and its licence covers mockups for Apple platforms. We ship its Z as outlines, in an icon that already ships (`zimi/assets/icon.png` carries the same glyph), and the generator reads the font only to take that outline. This is noted here so it is on record; nothing has been changed because of it.
